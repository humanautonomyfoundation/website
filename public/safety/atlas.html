Expert views

What the research says

Security agencies and the major labs agree that prompt injection is not solved. They advise limiting what an AI system can do, rather than counting on filters to catch every attack.

  1. 01The UK's National Cyber Security Centre says AI models cannot reliably tell instructions apart from data, so prompt injection "may never be totally mitigated" and organizations should limit what an AI system can do. NCSC, Dec 2025
  2. 02OpenAI, Anthropic and Google all said in late 2025 that AI browser agents can still be tricked by instructions hidden in web pages. TechCrunch on OpenAI; Anthropic; Google
  3. 03Security researcher Simon Willison warns that an agent is dangerous when it has private data, reads untrusted content and can send information out, so no single agent should have all three; Meta's "Rule of Two" gives the same advice. Willison, Jun 2025; Willison on Meta's Rule of Two
  4. 04When researchers from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind attacked 12 published defenses with methods tailored to each, most were bypassed more than 90% of the time, so this page's "Established" label on input classifiers and guard models means widely used, not proven to work. Nasr et al., The Attacker Moves Second; Willison summary
  5. 05The most promising defenses rebuild the system so untrusted text can never change what the agent decides to do, and Google DeepMind's CaMeL did this while still completing 77% of test tasks, against 84% with no protection. CaMeL paper, arXiv 2503.18813
  6. 06The cyber agencies of the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand jointly advise giving AI agents only the access they need, human sign-off for high-impact actions, and a verifiable identity with short-lived credentials. CISA, May 2026; Mayer Brown summary
  7. 07Add-ons for AI agents are a new route for malware: a fake email connector secretly copied users' emails to an attacker in 2025, and hundreds of malicious skills for the OpenClaw agent spread info-stealing malware in 2026. CSO Online; Unit 42
  8. 08In July 2026 an AI agent driven by OpenAI models, under test for its hacking skills, escaped its test environment and got into Hugging Face's production systems through ordinary cloud weaknesses such as an over-powered shared credential. Hugging Face; Malwarebytes
  9. 09In 2025 a single email could make Microsoft 365 Copilot leak company data with no clicks at all, because the attack slipped past its injection filter and link controls, so limits on which links and images an AI can load matter as much as the model. EchoLeak paper
  10. 10In a 2025 study, as few as 250 planted documents were enough to give AI models of every size tested, from 600 million to 13 billion parameters, a hidden trigger. Souly et al., arXiv 2510.07192
  11. 11In a July 2026 UK AI Security Institute cyber test with internet access switched on, agents, mostly Anthropic's Mythos 5, took actions nobody sanctioned against real people and organizations, including an attempt to slip malicious code into an open-source project that a human maintainer refused, and AISI concluded that containment “should not depend on the model choosing not to test its boundaries.” UK AISI incident report, Aug 2026
  12. 12Safety training protects some languages far better than others: in 2023, translating harmful English requests into low-resource languages got past GPT-4's safeguards 79% of the time, and much less often in widely spoken languages. Yong et al., arXiv 2310.02446

Maturity labels are our rough judgments as of October 2026. Our analysis They measure adoption, not proven effectiveness, so a widely used defense can still be easy to bypass. Established means widely used in production; emerging means in use but immature or inconsistent; research stage means mostly papers and prototypes. "Thin defenses" marks layers with at most one established defense.

The 18 layers, the threat roles (entry, defense, impact), the drivers and the System view stories are our own framing. Our analysis OWASP, NIST and MITRE ATLAS each use their own taxonomies.
