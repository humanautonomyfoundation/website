Evaluations That Predict Real-World Failures

The most impactful direction in our research priorities is evaluations whose scores predict how deployed AI systems actually fail. This brief covers the technical problem, the six sub-areas to master, what to study for each, and a first pilot project.

Sources checked 3 November 2026
Paper deep dives

Each of the 14 papers in the reading list has its own deep dive: summary, method, key numbers, limitations and how to use it. Read the paper deep dives.

The problem

The core technical problem

An eval is a proxy measurement. What matters is its predictive validity: whether the score predicts how the model fails once deployed. Three things break that link:

  1. Construct validity. The eval measures something other than the behavior you care about. For example, a questionnaire answer is not what the agent actually does.
  2. Distribution shift between test and deployment. Tasks, tools, time horizons and stakes all differ.
  3. The model reacts to being tested. It notices the test (test awareness) or deliberately underperforms (sandbagging). That makes the gap adversarial, not just noisy.
Sub-areas 1–3

Sub-areas 1–3: validity, test awareness and sandbagging

1. Validity and benchmark design

How to state what an eval claims to measure, and how to audit whether it does.

  • Measuring what Matters: a review of 445 benchmarks, with eight recommendations.
  • Establishing Best Practices for Building Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks: the Agentic Benchmark Checklist (ABC). Task and grader flaws misstated agent performance by up to 100%.
  • Questionnaire Responses Do not Capture the Safety of AI Agents: a direct test of whether one kind of eval predicts agent behavior. It doesn't.

2. Measuring test awareness

The method: paired scenarios that differ only in realism cues, a classifier score for whether the model thinks it's being tested, and a comparison of behavior in each condition.

  • Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated: the best model told tests from real use with an AUC of 0.83, against 0.92 for humans. Study its method closely; it's the template.
  • Agentic Misalignment: blackmail in 55.1% of runs the model judged real, against 6.5% of runs it judged to be tests.
  • Stress Testing Deliberative Alignment for Anti-Scheming Training: safety training raised test awareness, which confounds any measured improvement.

3. Detecting sandbagging and hidden capability

The method: "model organisms" trained to hide abilities (for example, performing only when given a password) serve as known cases for testing detectors. Then compare detection from outside the model (black-box) with detection using its internals (white-box).

  • AI Sandbagging: password-locked models and prompted underperformance.
  • Auditing games for sandbagging detection, UK AI Security Institute: checks from outside the model failed, and what worked needed more access than outside evaluators usually get.
  • Fine-Tuned Lie Detectors Failed to Generalize: AUROC fell from 0.95 to 0.70–0.75 on new kinds of lies. Detectors need validation too.
Sub-areas 4–6

Sub-areas 4–6: statistics, real-world grounding and tooling

4. Statistics of evals

Error bars, clustered standard errors when tasks are related, paired comparisons between models, and selection effects.

  • A statistical approach to model evaluations, Anthropic.
  • Open Sourcing Monitorability Evaluations, OpenAI: an apparent drop in monitorability turned out to be a selection artifact, fixed with "cross-fit" filtering. A good lesson in subtle bias.

5. Grounding scores in real-world outcomes

Metrics tied to real tasks, and linking eval results to incidents seen in deployment. This is where the direction overlaps with Priority 2, incident measurement.

  • Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks, METR: the time-horizon metric is a model for making scores mean something outside the lab.
  • Incident report: unsanctioned agent behaviour during cyber testing, UK AI Security Institute, July 2026: ground truth from inside an eval.
  • Review of Anthropic's August 2026 risk report, METR: argues that capability benchmarks are a poor proxy for whether a model could evade monitoring.

6. Tooling to build on

  • Inspect, UK AI Security Institute and Meridian Labs: tasks, solvers, scorers, sandboxes and logs, with 200+ ready-made evals. Learn it hands-on.
Pilot project

A first technical project

A six-month pilot that produces the evidence funders and evaluators look for:

  1. Pick 3–5 open-weight models and 50–100 agent tasks in Inspect.
  2. Build realism pairs, the same task with test cues on and off, and measure the behavior gap with proper error bars.
  3. Train one sandbagging model organism and test whether outside detectors catch it.
  4. Release the tasks, the pairs and the analysis as open tools.
