Who's driving it

Four kinds of risk, four kinds of defense Our analysis

The driver decides which defenses work. Against bad actors you protect the AI from people; against a misaligned AI you protect people from the AI.

These four drivers closely match Google DeepMind's 2025 taxonomy: misuse, misalignment, mistakes and structural risks. The International AI Safety Report 2026 uses three groups instead: misuse, malfunctions and systemic risks.

EstablishedEmergingResearch onlyThe bars and dots are our rough ratings Our analysis

Maturity is uneven. Our analysis Defenses against misuse and accidents are the most mature. Defenses against a misaligned AI are mostly still research. Structural risks are widely discussed but have no clear owner. No source measures attention by risk type, so the attention bars are our judgment. Frontier labs focus on misuse and misalignment (Google DeepMind). Published frameworks most often cover AI failures and social and economic harm, and only about 7% cover risks from many AI agents interacting (MIT AI Risk Repository).

Coverage

Which defenses cover which risks Our analysis

Some defenses only cover half the problem. Refusal-style safeguards stop a person asking for help, but do little against a model with goals of its own. That needs alignment training plus outside monitoring (Google DeepMind). In our view, human gatekeepers are the closest thing to a universal defense: no column where they fail outright. Not everyone agrees. Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor prefer auditing, monitoring, least privilege and undoable actions over constant human sign-off (AI as Normal Technology).

Where to contribute

Gaps a small research group could own Editor’s focus Our analysis

Revised on 31 October 2026. The first six cards follow our ranking of research priorities: three priorities, an alternative for each kind of funder, and four more areas. The last two are earlier ideas we still track.

Priority 1Evals that predict real-world failures

Build tests whose results match how agents behave in deployment, including models that act differently when they know they're being tested.

Priority 2Incident measurement and safety cases

Standardize how agent incidents are recorded, collect exposure figures such as agent-hours, and test whether safety cases predict fewer incidents. This needs data-sharing partners.

Priority 3Safety that survives updates

Check whether safety holds after fine-tuning, quantization, model merging, memory changes, and reinforcement learning with reward hacking.

Alternative · catastrophic-risk fundersControl and monitoring under pressure

Simulate oversight protocols and graduated permissions for agents, and measure what a trusted monitor actually catches under adversarial pressure, including agents colluding.

Alternative · near-term fundersEnforceable tool-use boundaries

Publish open, repeatable tests of how well deployers' protections hold up against prompt injection. Test enforceable tool limits too: information-flow control, capability-limited tools and authorization checks.

Also worth fundingFour more areas

Safety of automated AI research and development; verification of compute and international agreements; multilingual safety and unequal protection across languages; privacy, memory leakage and durable unlearning.

Earlier ideaFleet command limits Our analysis

We found no standard aimed at limiting what one controller can order a fleet of cars or robots to do. Vehicle cybersecurity and software updates are already regulated under UN R155/R156 and ISO/SAE 21434, so this work would sit alongside that community.

Earlier ideaMeasure structural drift

Track how much critical decision-making depends on AI, so slow loss of control becomes visible.

Expert views

What the research says

Experts broadly agree that AI already causes real harm and that safety testing is not keeping up. They disagree, by a factor of 10 to 100 or more, on the odds of catastrophe.

  1. 01This page's four drivers closely follow Google DeepMind's split into misuse, misalignment, mistakes and structural risks, while the International AI Safety Report puts loss of control and everyday failures together as “malfunctions.” Google DeepMind, 2025; International AI Safety Report 2026
  2. 02In the largest survey of AI researchers (2,778 people, 2023), between 38% and 51% gave at least a 10% chance of outcomes as bad as human extinction, yet 68% thought good outcomes were more likely than bad. Grace et al., Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI
  3. 03Professional forecasters are far less worried than AI experts: in a 2022 tournament, experts put the chance of AI-caused human extinction by 2100 at 3% and superforecasters at 0.38%. 80,000 Hours on the Forecasting Research Institute tournament
  4. 04Debate barely closes that gap: after eight weeks, worried experts' median chance of an AI-caused existential catastrophe by 2100 fell only from 25% to 20%, and skeptics' rose only from 0.10% to 0.12%. Forecasting Research Institute
  5. 05The 2026 International AI Safety Report, written by over 100 experts and chaired by Yoshua Bengio, says some researchers see loss of control as a serious possibility while others find it implausible, and that pre-release safety tests do not reliably predict real-world risk. International AI Safety Report 2026
  6. 06Experts expected AI to win gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad about five years later than it did, but in a small trial a language model gave people no measurable help with biology lab tasks. The Decoder on the Forecasting Research Institute's expert panel
  7. 07The UN's new scientific panel on AI warned in July 2026 that current safeguards cannot keep pace with the growth of AI's capabilities. UN press release
  8. 08In April 2025, Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton told CBS there is a “sort of 10 to 20%” chance that AI systems could eventually seize control. Yahoo News on Hinton's CBS interview
  9. 09Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor of Princeton call catastrophic misalignment “(by far) the most speculative” of the risks they discuss, which runs against this page's red highlight on “the AI itself.” AI as Normal Technology
  10. 10Skeptics make different points: 76% of 475 researchers in a 2025 AAAI survey doubt that scaling up today's methods will reach human-level AI, and Timnit Gebru argues that extinction talk distracts from harms happening now. The Decoder on the AAAI survey; AI Weekly on Gebru's WIRED interview
  11. 11An October 2026 review rated the evidence for loss of control from AI agents only “weakly plausible.” That describes how strong the evidence is, not a probability. No study met the validity bar for a stronger finding, and gaps in external validity were widespread. Uuk et al., 2026
  12. 12Asking a model how it would act in a made-up scenario does not reliably predict how an agent built on it behaves, so questionnaire-style safety tests say little about real-world risk. Hellrigel-Holderbaum and Young, 2026
