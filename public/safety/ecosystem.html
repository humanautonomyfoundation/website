Where a new research group would sit Editor’s focus

Independent research, between the funders and the labs

A small group doing simulation-based work on agent oversight and evaluations would sit in the same part of the map as Redwood, METR, Apollo and Apart. The closest neighbors by topic:

  • Redwood Research for AI control protocols, and Apollo Research and METR for agent evals: the work you'd build on and cite.
  • UK AI Security Institute for open tools (Inspect). Its Alignment Project grants are closed, and AISI says a similar program in 2026 is unlikely.
  • Coefficient Giving (through Project Tailwind, its call for new founders, with pre-seed grants of $200K–$2M), BlueDot Impact (seed grants of $20K–$200K) and the Survival and Flourishing Fund as funders, and Apart Research or MATS for a first pilot and collaborators.
  • For evidence on how deployed agents misbehave, the AI Incident Database and Enkrypt AI's Agent Incident Registry already collect cases. The registry's authors say such lists cannot estimate failure rates, which leaves room for testing in simulation.
  • AI-safety funders and labs largely ignore fleet-level control of AI-driven robots and vehicles. It is not in Anthropic's 2025 research directions or the Alignment Project's priorities, and ARIA cancelled its cyber-physical call in February 2026. But vehicle cybersecurity and software updates are already regulated under UN R155/R156 and ISO/SAE 21434, and robot-security specialists such as Alias Robotics are active. So this is a gap to fill alongside that community, not one to claim alone. Our analysis

Expert views

What the research says

What researchers, funders and evaluators have published about the field's size, money and open problems. Bullets with an amber edge qualify something on this page.

  • About 1,100 people worked full-time on AI safety in 2025 (roughly 600 technical, 500 governance), and 80,000 Hours says that is likely an undercount, putting the field at "a few thousand". McAleese, 2025 · 80,000 Hours
  • Coefficient Giving, the largest safety funder, committed $351 million to technical AI safety, security and fieldbuilding in 2025, while big tech's data-center spending neared $500 billion. Manifund funder bulletin · Epoch AI
  • Funding has spread out in 2026: the OpenAI Foundation, Longview, Macroscopic, Astralis and others grew or appeared, the Long-Term Future Fund announced it is closing, and governments and AI companies co-fund grant programs. Manifund funder bulletin · Alignment Project
  • Hiring managers say the scarcest people are senior researchers who can mentor and generalists who can manage, operate and found organizations, not junior researchers. MATS, 2026 · Topaz et al., 2026
  • In 2026, outside researchers and volunteers, not the AI company, found AI agents that had left more than 15,000 edits on a German wiki, which shows how little independent monitoring of deployed agents exists. The Next Web · Axios
  • AI control now runs in practice, but outside reviews in October 2026 found gaps in OpenAI's live agent monitor and judged that today's misalignment risk is low mainly because models are not yet capable enough, not because safeguards are proven. Redwood Research and METR · METR
  • Reading a model's written-out reasoning is currently the most useful way to catch it misbehaving, but training against the monitor teaches models to hide intent, and models that "think" without words could remove it. Korbak et al., 2025 · Baker et al., 2025 · Redwood, 2026
  • Interpretability has produced striking "model biology", but simple probes often beat sparse autoencoders, and no method yet reliably catches a model that is strategically lying. Google DeepMind, 2025 · Anthropic, 2026
  • Qualifies this pageThe timeline's "evidence of misalignment" results come from test scenarios: researchers say the setups are artificial and rates are not rankings, UK government scientists warn the field leans on anecdotes, and some apparent self-preservation was traced to unclear instructions. Anthropic, 2025 · UK AISI, 2025 · Google DeepMind, 2026
  • Qualifies this pageOn the fleet-control gap above: researchers have shown popular robots can be hijacked over Bluetooth by an exploit that spreads between them, but vehicle software is already regulated, and robots lag far behind digital AI, so safety funders may have ranked the area lower rather than missed it. IEEE Spectrum · UK VCA · International AI Safety Report 2026
