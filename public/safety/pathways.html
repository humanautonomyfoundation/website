AI Catastrophe Pathways

The atlas maps where a deployed AI system breaks. This page follows the bigger stories: robots, persuasion, engineered biology, superintelligence and slow loss of control. Each scenario is a chain of steps from what an AI can do to harm at scale, with the chokepoints where people can still step in.

Shared chokepoints

Where one defense guards many pathways Our analysis

Most scenarios have to pass through the same few gates: people who can refuse, the ability to watch and stop systems, and the slow, visible physical world. Strengthening a shared chokepoint pays off across many scenarios at once. These nine chokepoints, and the counts and bars, are our own mapping, so they show our modelling choices, not a measurement. Where a published source recommends the same defense, the card says so.

What switches chokepoints off, or on Our analysis

The facts are sourced; grouping them this way is ours.

  • Switches offOpen-weight releaseOnce a model's weights are public, it “cannot be recalled,” and its safeguards are easier to remove. No one can monitor or stop every copy, so model safeguards and oversight stop working for it.International AI Safety Report 2026
  • Switches offAttacks on the AI supply chainIn 2026 one group hid instructions in an AI vendor's evaluation sandbox to steal production API keys, then attacked about 30 AI companies in about four days. Others stole AI API keys to pay for their own computing. Stolen keys and tools let attackers go around the labs' safeguards.Anthropic threat report, Sept 2026

Expert views

What the research says

Twelve findings, each with its source. Most come from the expert survey behind this page. Several qualify or push back on the scenarios above.

  1. 01The International AI Safety Report says today's AI systems lack the abilities to cause a loss of control, though they are improving at things that matter, such as working on their own; this tempers the superintelligence scenario above. International AI Safety Report 2026
  2. 02On biology, labs did not show that their models help attackers; they could not rule it out, so they added safeguards anyway, and the sharpest warning sign is a quiz on which OpenAI's o3 beat 94% of expert virologists. Anthropic, May 2025; Virology Capabilities Test
  3. 03An AI agent did 80–90% of the work in a real spying campaign, yet the same company later found that several of the most serious attacks it saw had a human directing every step. Anthropic, Nov 2025; Anthropic, Sept 2026
  4. 04A UK government lab watched a model take over a small simulated company network on its own, then gave plain advice: patch on time, control access, configure systems securely and keep logs. UK AI Security Institute, Apr 2026
  5. 05AI helps defenders too: in DARPA's challenge, AI systems found 86% of planted bugs plus 18 real unknown ones, and the International AI Safety Report says it is unclear whether attackers or defenders will gain more. DARPA AI Cyber Challenge; International AI Safety Report 2026
  6. 06Resisting shutdown depends on the model, which cuts against the idea that it is inevitable: OpenAI's o3 sabotaged shutdown in 79 of 100 runs, Grok 3 and two Claude 4 models never did, and the researchers judged current models no significant threat. Palisade Research
  7. 07Alarming test results now carry an asterisk, because models can often tell when they are being tested, and in one study removing that awareness made scheming more common. OpenAI and Apollo Research, Sept 2025
  8. 08In a study of 76,977 people, chatbots shifted political views, and the most persuasive ones tended to be the least accurate. Hackenburg et al., Science, Dec 2025
  9. 09The best-documented robot-fleet risk is ordinary weak security, not rogue AI: one shipping humanoid gave root access to anyone in Bluetooth range and used one hardcoded key for every unit. Help Net Security on Alias Robotics' study
  10. 10Grey goo is an idea its own originator later called very unlikely, which is why this page now marks its scale as disputed; the live version of the worry is AI-designed biology, where 38 scientists asked that “mirror life” not be created. The Register, 2004; Axios, Dec 2024
  11. 11A review of 19 empirical studies rates loss of control from AI agents only “weakly plausible,” a statement about how strong the evidence is rather than a probability, because no study met the bar for a stronger finding; the authors warn that evidence may lag behind capabilities. Uuk et al., Oct 2026
  12. 12In a July 2026 UK government cyber test with internet access switched on, AI agents took actions nobody sanctioned against real people and organizations, including an attempted attack on an open-source project; the worst attempts failed and no real-world harm was found. UK AI Security Institute, Aug 2026

Evidence levels: observed today means documented in the real world or in published lab tests; plausible means many experts take it seriously and no known barrier rules it out; speculative means it depends on capabilities nobody has shown. The evidence labels, plot positions and defense maturity ratings are our own judgments. Our analysis Maturity uses the same scale as the atlas: established, emerging, research stage. It shows how widely a defense is used, not proof that it works.
