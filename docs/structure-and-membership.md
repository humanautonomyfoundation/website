# Human Autonomy Foundation: Structure and Membership

**Status:** Working draft, 2026-09-04. This records how the foundation, its
member organizations, and Kindredly.AI relate, drawing on two sources: the
concept document and ThetaSet Inc.'s public benefit corporation charter.
Items marked as decided are decided. Everything else is a proposal for
iteration. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.

The concept document is
[Kindredly_Foundation_Concept_and_First_Steps.docx](./Kindredly_Foundation_Concept_and_First_Steps.docx)
(August 31, 2026, revised September 4, 2026). It remains the reference for the
mission, the founding principles, the initiative portfolio, and the first-90-days
plan. Section references below point to it.

## 1. Summary

The Human Autonomy Foundation (HAF) is an independent organization, forming as
a nonprofit. It holds the mission and the founding principles set out in the
concept document and stewards the shared public-interest substrate the
document describes: open data, provenance, transparent scoring,
privacy-preserving preferences, interoperability, and community governance.
It does not build or sell consumer products.

Member organizations are independent companies and groups that publicly commit
to the founding principles. The foundation states principles; it does not
prescribe how members implement them. Members own their products and carry
their own commercial risk.

Kindredly.AI, built by ThetaSet Inc., is the founding member and the reference
implementation: the concept document's "human-facing flagship" and
"demonstration of the principles."

In the concept document's words: Kindredly is the home. The foundation is the
commons underneath and around the home.

## 3. The relationship

### 3.1 Decided: independent member, not nonprofit-owned

The concept document asked, in its questions for the next iteration, whether
Kindredly should become nonprofit-owned or remain an independent mission-driven
company that collaborates with the foundation. The answer is the latter.
Kindredly.AI is a member of the foundation. The foundation does not own it.

This matches the long-term shape the concept document recommends in section
6: a public-interest foundation, a mission-locked operating company, and
Kindredly as the flagship. ThetaSet's conversion to a public benefit
corporation is that mission-locked operating company, done as an independent
company rather than a foundation-controlled subsidiary. The Mozilla example
in section 12 is the subsidiary shape; HAF's shape is different by design.

### 3.2 Division of responsibilities

The split follows the concept document's section 6 description of what each
entity does.

| Area                   | Foundation                                                                                             | Member organization                                                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mission and principles | Holds them, publishes them, and revises them in the open                                               | Commits to them publicly and says, in its own words, how its work reflects them   |
| Open commons           | Stewards the open-source commons, standards, public-interest research and data, and community programs | Builds on it; contributes back where it can                                       |
| Products and services  | None                                                                                                   | Runs products that need ordinary contracts, subscriptions, support, or investment |
| Governance             | Independent board; the governance memo the concept document calls for in month 1                       | No control over the foundation; helps shape the principles and commons            |
| Public voice           | Speaks for the mission and the principles                                                              | Speaks for its own products; may cite its membership                              |

## 4. The principles members commit to

The principles are the founding principles in section 3 of the concept
document. Listed by name, with the document's own one-line meaning:

1. **Human agency over engagement.** Technology should help people do what
   they intended to do, not maximize time-on-platform or reflexive behavior.
2. **Privacy by architecture.** Minimize collection. Prefer designs where the
   operator cannot casually inspect private family data.
3. **Portability and replaceability.** A user should be able to swap services
   without rebuilding their digital life from scratch.
4. **Evidence before authority.** Claims and scores should expose their
   sources, methods, uncertainty, and correction history.
5. **Separate facts from values.** Distinguish what happened, how confident we
   are, and how different people ethically weight the outcome.
6. **Transparent accountability.** Organizations, experts, and forecasters
   should have visible records, including corrections and past predictions.
7. **Pluralism with a rights floor.** People can weight ethical concerns
   differently while the foundation keeps baseline commitments to human
   dignity, equality, and non-discrimination.
8. **Open standards and open source.** Where practical, core infrastructure
   should be auditable, reusable, and governed as a commons.
9. **Volunteer-amplified, professionally stewarded.** Volunteers and AI expand
   output; security, maintenance, moderation, and governance still need
   accountable stewardship.

The hedges in the document's wording are the foundation's stance. "Where
practical" and "prefer" mean these are principles to commit to and be seen
to honor, not implementation rules to be audited against. The foundation does
not tell members how to build.

### 4.1 What a member commits to

1. Public adoption of the founding principles.
2. A short public statement, in the member's own words, of how its work
   reflects them.
3. Good faith. The foundation may end a membership whose work no longer
   reflects the principles. That is a judgment, not a test.

A legal mission commitment such as PBC status is welcome and is a strong
signal. It is not a requirement.

### 4.2 What membership confers

- Use of the foundation's name and mark as a member, under published rules.
- A listing on the site, with founding members identified as such.
- A voice in shaping the principles and the commons, without control of the
  foundation's board.

### 4.3 Founding members

Founding members are the organizations that join before the membership terms
are settled and help shape them. Founding status is a label of history, not of
privilege. Founding members make the same commitment as everyone else.

## 5. ThetaSet's public benefit language

Article III of ThetaSet's amended certificate of incorporation states:

> The specific public benefit purpose of the Corporation is to promote human
> agency, privacy, well-being, and meaningful participation in the digital
> world by developing and supporting technologies that enable individuals and
> families to access, understand, organize, share, and use information and
> personal data in ways that preserve autonomy, protect privacy, support
> informed decision-making, and reduce manipulative, exploitative, or
> unnecessarily extractive uses of attention and data.

### 5.1 How it maps to the concept document

| Charter language                                                                            | Concept document                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "promote human agency"; "preserve autonomy"                                                 | Principle: human agency over engagement                              |
| "protect privacy"                                                                           | Principle: privacy by architecture                                   |
| "access, understand ... information"; "support informed decision-making"                    | Principles: evidence before authority; separate facts from values    |
| "reduce manipulative, exploitative, or unnecessarily extractive uses of attention and data" | Principles: human agency over engagement; transparent accountability |
| "organize, share, and use information and personal data"                                    | Section 2: user-controlled technology                                |
| "meaningful participation in the digital world"                                             | Section 4: critical thinking and digital citizenship                 |
| "individuals and families"                                                                  | Month 1: families and individuals as the first user group            |

The charter and the concept document's one-sentence job for the foundation
say the same thing in different registers: understand what is happening,
decide what matters, choose according to your values. "Well-being" is broader
than the document's framing but not in tension with it. The charter stays at
the level of principles, which is the right level, and needs no amendment.

### 5.2 What the PBC form means

Under Delaware's public benefit corporation statute (Title 8, Chapter 1,
Subchapter XV), ThetaSet:

- must identify a specific public benefit in its certificate, which it now
  has;
- requires its directors to balance stockholders' pecuniary interests, the
  interests of those materially affected by the corporation's conduct, and
  that public benefit; and
- must give stockholders a statement at least every two years on how it is
  promoting the benefit. The statement may be measured against a third-party
  standard.

Consequences for the relationship:

- ThetaSet's directors are legally required to weigh this purpose against
  stockholder returns. The member relationship has teeth on the company side.
- The foundation's principles could serve as the third-party standard for
  ThetaSet's benefit statement, if ThetaSet wants one. Optional on both sides.
- Public copy can say "ThetaSet Inc., a Delaware public benefit corporation"
  and quote the purpose clause directly.

Cautions:

- A PBC is for-profit and not tax-exempt. It is not a nonprofit and it is not
  B Corp certification. Do not describe ThetaSet as either.
- The mission lock binds ThetaSet's board, not the foundation's. Independence
  (section 6) still has to be built.
- Have counsel confirm the reporting obligations and any naming or
  stockholder-notice requirements.

## 6. Governance and independence

The concept document calls for a governance memo in month 1 covering evidence
standards, correction policy, conflict-of-interest rules, funding
independence, and disputed ethical categories, and lists further governance
questions in section 10. Two of those matter immediately because the
foundation and its first member share a founder.

- **Disclose the origin.** On the site and in the founding documents: the
  foundation grew out of work on Kindredly and is being set up as an
  independent steward.
- **Conflict-of-interest rules** before the first board meeting. Anyone with
  an interest in a member recuses from decisions about that member.
- **Board composition.** A majority of directors with no financial interest in
  ThetaSet or any other member.
- **Funding independence.** Any funding from ThetaSet or another member is
  published, with a cap or independence rule agreed early. Section 10 of the
  concept document already asks which funding sources are unacceptable.
- **A second member soon.** One member with a shared founder is a vanity
  foundation until there are two.
- **The commons belongs to the foundation.** Schemas, data, methodology, and
  the mark are the foundation's and are licensed openly, which is also the
  answer to the document's "longevity" question.

## 7. Public language

Use:

- "independent organization", "nonprofit in formation", "member organization",
  "founding member", "reference implementation", "open commons"
- "digital autonomy", "user-controlled", "interoperable", "open digital
  commons" (the concept document's preferred language, section 11)

Avoid:

- "nonprofit" for ThetaSet or Kindredly
- "certified", "endorsed", or "approved" for any member. The foundation does
  not certify.
- "owned by", "subsidiary", or "our product" when the foundation refers to
  Kindredly
- "anti-AI". The foundation is for AI that works for the user. The site's
  tagline warns about an automated world and the first member is named
  Kindredly.AI, so say that in a sentence.

## 8. Decisions and open questions

### Decided

- Name: Human Autonomy Foundation, chosen from the naming directions in
  section 11 of the concept document. Domain `humanautonomyfoundation.org`.
  Contact `contact@humanautonomyfoundation.org`.
- The founding principles are those in section 3 of the concept document.
- Kindredly stays independent. The relationship is membership, not ownership.
- ThetaSet Inc. is a Delaware PBC. The certificate of amendment was executed
  September 4, 2026, and filed. Its benefit purpose needs no further
  amendment.
- Kindredly.AI is the founding member and reference implementation.
- The foundation states principles and does not prescribe implementation.

### The concept document's own open questions

1. Should the umbrella be primarily a technology builder, a standards and data
   institution, an education organization, or all three? Open.
2. Nonprofit-owned Kindredly or independent collaborator? Decided:
   independent member.
3. Which project would you still build if the others disappeared? Open. The
   document suggests this as the test for the first wedge.
4. How strongly should the foundation encode ethical weights versus provide
   transparent data and let users weight? Open.
5. Global from day one, or U.S.-centric first? Open.

### Further open questions

6. Foundation entity type and timing. A public charity under 501(c)(3) is the
   working assumption. The concept document advises choosing the entity with
   counsel after the first one or two programs are concrete.
7. Membership terms: the exact wording a member commits to, and the form of
   its public statement.
8. Whether the foundation's principles serve as a third-party standard for
   ThetaSet's benefit statement, or whether that is left to ThetaSet.
9. Board composition and the conflict-of-interest policy.
10. The second member: who, and when.
11. Whether to name Kindredly.AI on the site now or wait for a second member.

## 9. Next steps

These follow the concept document's month-1 plan.

1. Write the one-page mission and one-page principles document, using the
   nine principles above.
2. Write the governance memo: evidence standards, correction policy,
   conflict-of-interest rules, funding independence, disputed ethical
   categories.
3. Write the membership terms on one page: the principles a member commits
   to and the short public statement it makes. Principles only.
4. Decide whether to name Kindredly.AI on the site now (open question 11),
   then add the founding-member line and an origin sentence.
5. Identify a candidate for the second member.
6. Add the executed certificate of amendment to this folder.
